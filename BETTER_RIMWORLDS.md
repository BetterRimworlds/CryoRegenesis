# Better Rimworlds: A Life Beyond One Colony

Better Rimworlds is an interconnected collection of mods built around a different
long-term question for RimWorld: **what if leaving a planet did not mean leaving
your people and their history behind?**

Its direction is not simply to make colonists harder to kill or give an established
colony more construction projects. It is to give long-lived people worthwhile
things to accomplish, consequences to live with, and new worlds on which to
continue their stories.

Together, CryoRegenesis, Stargate, PoluxKeeper, the proposed CorticalImmunity, and
the in-progress Emperors and Consuls connect personal survival, exploration,
ecological stewardship, and political responsibility.

## From Survival to Purpose

RimWorld already excels at making individual people matter. Injuries,
relationships, skills, disasters, and recoveries turn a collection of pawns into
a cast with a shared history.

Better Rimworlds builds on that attachment. Its technologies can extend the lives
of those characters and carry them beyond one settlement. Its longer campaigns
give them reasons to use that additional time.

The intended progression is not mandatory or strictly linear, but its parts fit
together:

1. Establish a colony and survive.
2. Develop the means to restore health and youth.
3. Become important to people beyond the colony.
4. Undertake projects measured in years or decades.
5. Carry the same people into another world.
6. Accept new responsibilities created by their previous achievements.

**A successful colony becomes the beginning of a larger story, rather than merely
the point at which there is nothing left to do.**

## CryoRegenesis: Restoring People

CryoRegenesis provides rejuvenation, extensive healing, and conditional
resurrection. Resurrection still has requirements and a lasting Luciferium cost;
the machine does not make a person impossible to kill.

Its narrative importance is larger than medical convenience. Long-lived colonists
can remain involved in projects whose results take decades. The person who built
the first treatment room can still be there when the Emperor arrives.

### Extraordinary technology, not ordinary healthcare

RimWorld already contains age reversal, cryptosleep, resurrector mech serum, and
sanguophages. Indefinite biological longevity is therefore not exclusive to this
collection, and longevity should not be confused with invulnerability.

What makes CryoRegenesis extraordinary is the combination of capabilities in a
repeatable installation: restoring youth, repairing bodies, and reversing recent
death using supplies a colony can obtain.

That supports an interpretation of **glitterworld engineering approaching
archotech-level outcomes**. A regenerative core based on poorly understood
archotech mechanisms would be a plausible lore explanation, but should be treated
as a possible framing rather than an established implementation detail.

For the Imperial story, the critical premise is not the technology's label. It is
whether the colony provides something its patrons cannot readily reproduce.

## Imperial Regeneration: Becoming Indispensable

CryoRegenesis's Royalty quest chain turns a private medical advantage into public
obligations. Planetary clients and increasingly important Imperial visitors arrive
for treatment under return contracts.

The colony must restore them, protect them, and send them home. Recruiting or
losing contracted clients breaks trust and resets progression. Successful
treatment can also earn Imperial Honor, connecting this new activity to Royalty's
existing title progression and bestowing ceremonies.

The campaign advances through planetary clients, lower nobility, the Stellarch,
and eventually the Emperor and court. Using existing world leaders and properly
titled nobles helps these visits feel like dealings with a persistent world,
rather than transactions involving interchangeable strangers.

### A tested, decades-long undertaking

The maintainer has completed three test campaigns. The fastest took approximately
15 years and the longest approximately 30 years. Around 15–25 years is the
intended general scale; failures and restarts can extend the undertaking beyond
30 years.

This duration makes rejuvenation relevant to the campaign itself. It also leaves
room for roads, exploration, cultivation, restoration, and other projects between
Imperial visits. The contract chain need not fill every moment if those years
have independent value.

### Power grows out of useful work

The colony earns attention because it does something the powerful want: it gives
them more life. Treatment facilities, uranium supplies, electrical reliability,
and visitor safety become parts of an institution with political significance.

This is a particularly effective way to extend core RimWorld gameplay. A powerful
machine solves some survival problems, but its success creates new obligations.
The question changes from “Can we keep ourselves alive?” to “Can we keep the
promises on which our influence depends?”

## Becoming Emperor

The Imperial endings allow the colony's relationship with power to culminate in
service, departure with the court, or betrayal.

The usurpation is especially closely tied to the underlying mechanics. The same
casket that restores the Emperor can, when unpowered, preserve him in cryptosleep
while a colony noble leaves with the court and is recognized as Emperor.

**The medical instrument becomes both a prison and the means of succession.**

The political interpretation is that years of service have made the colony
indispensable to influential people. Recognition by the Imperial household gives
an ambitious noble a basis for succession beyond simply possessing a weapon.
That reasoning should be communicated through the story, rather than assumed to
be self-evident.

Earlier research by the maintainer and assistant found no other mod or existing
route offering this particular opportunity to become Emperor. That is the basis
for describing it as a distinctive player experience, not a claim of an exhaustive
or permanently current survey of every RimWorld mod.

The deeper achievement is not merely adding a higher title. It is making that
title the outcome of the colony's accumulated history.

## Stargate: The People Continue

Stargate supplies the continuity that makes these achievements larger than one
save. It is not merely a transport building: its shared transfer buffer allows
people and items to leave one game and be recalled into another.

Its persistence systems address the details that make a returning character the
same character: relationships, royal titles, prisoner status, lived age,
CryoRegenesis history, and personal research memory.

This does not mean every connected planet is continuously simulated while the
player is elsewhere. The important continuity is that of the travelers and the
history they carry.

That changes the meaning of a new beginning. A new settlement need not require a
new cast. A veteran can arrive carrying centuries of experience and established
relationships, rather than being replaced by a newly generated pawn with similar
skills.

Atlantis progression also provides a concrete alternative to routine expansion:
research physically develops the facility through additions such as rich soil,
platforms, dining infrastructure, and Janus's laboratory. Discovery becomes a way
of rebuilding and inhabiting a place.

## PoluxKeeper: Restoring the Place

Long-lived people need more than the ability to survive. Ecological restoration
offers a constructive purpose for the additional years.

PoluxKeeper makes Polux trees easier to locate and monitor through growth
information, estimated growing time, tree cycling, and distant markers. When a
separate mod enables a suitable seed harvest, it also allows growers to collect
seeds automatically without cutting down the trees.

It does not itself accelerate growth or pollution removal. Its contribution is
to make stewardship manageable, not instantaneous.

The accompanying grove-statistics tool measures pollution, tree coverage, and
actual cleaning. Those measurements help turn a grove into a visible historical
achievement: land restored through a colony's sustained work.

Roads connect places. Groves improve them. Both give a mature colony reasons to
remain invested in its world while the Imperial campaign unfolds.

## CorticalImmunity: Preserving Recorded Identity

CorticalImmunity is a separate mod in development, intended to back up colonists
and prisoners onto “cookies,” drawing inspiration from Black Mirror's digital
copies and Altered Carbon's cortical stacks.

Its proposed role is to address losses CryoRegenesis cannot repair, particularly
destruction of the original head or brain.

The distinction matters:

- **Healing and rejuvenation** preserve the existing living person.
- **Conditional resurrection** restores someone whose remains meet the necessary
  requirements.
- **Backup restoration** would recover a recorded version of a mind.

The last possibility introduces questions rather than automatically answering
them. What happens to experiences acquired since the backup? Is a stored cookie
dormant information or capable of conscious experience? Is restoration personal
survival, duplication, or something more ambiguous?

Prisoner backups make consent and control especially important. Voluntary
insurance and an involuntary copy held by a captor have very different moral
meanings. These are potential design and storytelling questions, not claims that
particular restoration mechanics are already implemented.

## Emperors and Consuls: Power Becomes Responsibility

Emperors and Consuls is an in-progress, standalone sequel to the CryoRegenesis
Royalty endings. Its central proposal is to make the next chapter playable.

The court travels through Stargate, using the departing party recorded in
`Shuttle.xml`. On a new rimworld, a recognized Consul or Emperor enables the
political systems. The player does not simply choose an imperial starting
scenario and receive an invented history.

### Conquest is only the beginning

The planned expansion loop is:

1. Defeat an eligible settlement with a colony Knight or higher present.
2. Retain the settlement as an Imperial vassal rather than destroy it.
3. Station a Knight there through successive 60-day appointments.
4. Maintain continuous occupation for 300 days: five RimWorld years.
5. Send an Archon to incorporate the settlement into the Empire.
6. Recall the Archon or leave them as governor.

Existing Imperial cities are allies, not targets for vassalization. The design
uses settlement records and stationed world pawns rather than requiring a
player-controlled map for every holding.

### Expansion costs capable people

The strongest constraint is personal: a stationed Knight is absent from the home
colony, and one Knight cannot occupy several settlements simultaneously.

Expansion is therefore limited by the titled people the colony can spare, not
merely its stockpile of money. Nobility becomes administrative capacity. A title
means responsibility beyond its holder's privileges and accommodation demands.

Five years of occupation also distinguishes winning a battle from establishing
durable rule. Interrupted occupation can cause rebellion and reset progress.

### The succession crisis continues

The planned assassin continuation makes the promised sixty-day hunt playable on
the next world. A surviving marked claimant can become Emperor in that same save.
If a qualified killer inherits the mark, the full hunt restarts.

An ending's consequences thus become the next campaign's opening problem.

### Current status

The repository contains early foundations, including persisted recognition of a
claimant and Stargate-arrival title restoration. Its plan explicitly distinguishes
intended behaviour from implementation. The complete conquest, occupation, and
succession loops should not yet be presented as verified working features.

## What Holds the Collection Together

These mods address different forms of continuity:

| System | Its contribution |
|---|---|
| CryoRegenesis | Bodies survive aging, injury, and some deaths |
| Imperial regeneration contracts | Medical capability becomes reputation and influence |
| Stargate | Established people continue across worlds and saves |
| PoluxKeeper and cultivation | Sustained care improves the place they inhabit |
| CorticalImmunity, proposed | Recorded identity may survive otherwise irreversible loss |
| Emperors and Consuls, in progress | Earned political standing creates new responsibilities |

The collection's strength is the interaction between those ideas. Immortality
provides time. Travel provides new beginnings. Restoration provides constructive
purpose. Political advancement provides consequences that cannot be solved solely
by keeping one pawn alive.

## Design Principles Worth Preserving

- **Make additional years meaningful.** Long campaigns need intermediate
  achievements, not merely long waits.
- **Preserve people, not just statistics.** Relationships, memories, and history
  make continuity worth having.
- **Make power create obligations.** Medical miracles and noble titles should
  change what others expect from the colony.
- **Keep severe consequences understandable.** Losing years of trust can be a
  compelling disaster when the player could reasonably understand the risk.
- **Prefer decisions over repeated administration.** Renewing an appointment
  should matter because circumstances and people matter, not just because a timer
  expired.
- **Distinguish plans from working features.** The larger vision is strongest
  when its current capabilities and unfinished parts are described honestly.

## The Larger Promise

Better Rimworlds does not discard RimWorld's survival stories. It gives their
survivors somewhere to go next.

A colonist can help build a settlement, restore its visitors, survive decades of
Imperial service, leave with a court, and begin another chapter elsewhere. The
same people can build roads, cultivate groves, recover ancient facilities, and
eventually face the difficulty of governing what they have won.

**The aim is not merely to live forever. It is to have a world—and a continuing
story—worth living for.**
